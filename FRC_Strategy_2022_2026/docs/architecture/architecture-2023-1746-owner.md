# 2023-1746: One shared hand on a fixed inclined lift

Version: DCMP/Worlds rebuild as recalled by Jack Sarratt; exact installation dates unaudited

## Evidence premises

- **owner_firsthand_account**: Fixed approximately 30–45 degree four-stage elevator; shared cone/cube hand on a wrist; ground pickup and placement use that hand. Stage-count convention and angle reference remain unmeasured. Locator: Conversation supplied by Jack Sarratt, 2026-11-07
- **independent_source_partial_correspondence**: Later IRI interview describes a compact continuously rigged elevator and shared intake. It supports the family, not exact DCMP hardware identity. Locator: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5PhlmMCOir8&t=98

## Piece flow

Owner-described direct path: ground piece → roller hand → retained in that hand while wrist and carriage move → scoring release. Zero intermediate handoff on this described main path. Roller rotation is still a powered function; eliminating a handoff does not eliminate acquisition control.

## Physical arrangement and swept space

An inclined mast occupies a fixed diagonal corridor above the chassis. The carriage carries the wrist and hand along that corridor. The wrist swings the mouth toward the floor and then to a travel or placing orientation. The hand, held piece and any protruding cone sweep beyond the carriage; their clearance must be checked at every lift position. Mast feet must react overturning loads into the chassis. Battery access, exact mounting coordinates and measured extension envelope are not established.

## Independent motions and powered functions

Conditional kinematic model: one coordinated lift coordinate plus wrist rotation; roller spin handles piece transport. Four stages are not automatically four independently controlled degrees of freedom. Count drive translation/yaw separately and do not infer motor count from stages. Coupling/rigging details of the recalled configuration are unknown.

## Burdens avoided

The shared hand avoids transferring possession between a dedicated ground collector and scoring device on this path. A fixed mast avoids a powered shoulder angle. Fewer pose combinations can reduce collision planning and calibration work.

## Burdens retained

The driver must place the shared mouth close enough to the piece. Carriage stroke couples vertical height and horizontal reach. Mast compliance, stage friction, roller contact, cone orientation, wrist holding torque, loaded CG and driver alignment remain real costs. Low independent motion count does not establish easy fabrication or a light complete robot.

## Engineering hypothesis

If the task poses lie near the fixed mast trajectory and wrist reach, the architecture can obtain needed capability with fewer independent coordinates. Keeping possession in one hand removes an interface where timing, compression and support must otherwise agree. This is a plausible engineering mechanism, not a measured explanation of the season result.

## Rival explanation

Driver practice, autonomous execution, matchups, alliance partners and service quality can explain high results without proving architectural superiority. A more articulated robot might recover from more pickup orientations or reach a scoring pose with less chassis repositioning.

## When the preference reverses

The advantage reverses if floor capture requires slow precise alignment, mast/chassis positioning costs more time than a reliable handoff, or reach/stow/charge-station envelopes force bad routes. A wider collector can be worthwhile if acquisition dominates complete cycles.
