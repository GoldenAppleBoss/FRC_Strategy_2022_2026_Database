# 1833: independent ranking power and the cost of iteration

Owner correction, 2026-10-26. This instruction refines the existing elite-primary-contributor mandate. Physical iteration, fabrication, rebuilding and detailed CAD are expensive. Do not treat repeated hardware experiments as the default way to discover a good architecture. Front-load game understanding, spatial reasoning, interface analysis and the shortest useful calculations; reserve experiments for consequential uncertainty that could change the decision.

## Owner's four-lane example: preserve the actual chronology

The owner reports that 1833 did not finish fully CADding its two-lane shooter before changing to four. The team recognized that four lanes fit on the frame and adopted that opportunity. The stated decision chain was more lanes → more BPS → more throughput → more fuel scored → more ranking points → less dependence on partners → stronger seeding → more control over playoff alliance formation. This is an owner-reported packaging-led architecture decision, not a completed two-lane robot followed by a measured two-to-four-lane prototype campaign. No before/after test data, exact BPS multiplier or dimensions were supplied. Do not invent them or imply that the team wasted a finished build.

The transferable reasoning is to find inexpensive ways to raise the ceiling of the core scoring task early. If available space and a shared architecture allow more parallel scoring lanes at acceptable added cost, assess the clear gain promptly and make the decision before spending effort detailing the inferior package. An agent should recognize that opportunity, not require the team to fabricate both designs or finish both CAD assemblies to justify it.

Check the few interfaces that can invalidate the gain: available feed across the width, launch energy and electrical headroom, acquisition and inventory, shot validity/accuracy, physical clearance and added mass. This is targeted engineering reasoning, not an excuse to stall an evident improvement with a sprawling research program. Four lanes need not mean exactly twice sustained match output: the owner's qualitative decision chain is preserved while unmeasured multipliers remain unknown. Translate the available parallel capacity into the RP-relevant outcome under explicitly stated assumptions.

## First priority: ranking objectives 1833 can achieve independently

Design essential scoring and prerequisite capability so 1833 can pursue the most valuable independently achievable ranking bonuses without another robot completing required actions. Never make 'two capable partners will handle it' the justification for omitting a core prerequisite. Evaluate the robot's individual acquisition, autonomous and sustained scoring ceiling before relying on ideal alliance synergy. This reinforces the no-support-only architecture rule; it does not require every available mechanism or allow a turret.

For every ranking objective in the actual manual, record the exact conditions and classify it as: independently achievable; own contribution only with an explicit external prerequisite; or inherently dependent on another team/opponent. State which actions the robot owns, which are external, and whether the remaining dependency is controllable. A cooperation condition requiring an opposing alliance action cannot honestly be called solo-achievable. Win/tie RP also depends on relative alliance/opponent output; distinguish independently satisfiable bonus conditions from a claim of guaranteed match victory.

Map each solo-achievable objective into required auto contribution, teleop contribution, sustained valid throughput, time/supply assumptions and reliability margin. State attainable limits and shortfall explicitly. Use the actual rule thresholds and phase multipliers; do not copy 2026 thresholds into 2027 or invent unavailable game information. When thresholds permit different routes, compare the simplest route that preserves elite primary contribution. Once a threshold is secure, assess additional output for wins, tiebreakers and playoff ceiling rather than accumulating irrelevant features.

Compare candidates with two passive or weak partners for their independent bonus capability and direct output. Also analyze ordinary and elite partners for ranking/win potential and playoff interactions. The passive-partner stress case is an engineering diagnostic, not a literal claim that every alliance partner will contribute nothing. Do not double count partner output as 1833's ability. Report both threshold capability and confidence/range under defended, disrupted and late-match conditions.

The objective is strong ranking performance that gives the team more control over alliance selection, alongside an elite playoff robot. Seeding first and choosing preferred partners remain goals, not guarantees: opponent strength, RP rules, tiebreakers, scheduling, failures and draft availability also matter. Do not use those uncertainties to lower the stated ambition or recommend dependence on a specialist partner.

## Spend validation effort where it can change the choice

Use the cheapest adequate evidence in this order where applicable: governing rules and existing source data; rough frame/envelope layout and interference checks; simple rate, energy, cycle and volume bounds; existing mechanisms or representative interface tests; a narrowly scoped prototype; then integrated loaded validation when required before operation. This is a menu scaled to the uncertainty, not a command to perform every stage or endlessly iterate. Prototype evidence can be essential when friction, piece deformation or stochastic flow cannot be bounded adequately. Do not label a calculation a performed experiment, or ship an untested reliability claim.

For each proposed test, identify: the uncertainty, the competing decisions it can separate, the result that would change the choice, estimated money/CAD/fabrication/person-hours and schedule cost, cheapest available substitute, and a stopping rule. If all plausible outcomes lead to the same choice, explain why the test is not worth doing for architecture selection. Commissioning or required safety/legality checks may still be necessary for operating hardware; distinguish that purpose from open-ended design exploration. Unknown costs must be labeled rather than invented.

For a major architecture choice, retain the comparison and game model required by the design-review protocol. For a bounded improvement such as recognizing additional lane space, a short decision note with the old/new package, marginal cost, decisive interface checks and RP/throughput consequence can suffice. Do not demand full competing CAD models, fabricated designs or a large simulation to re-prove unchanged parts of the robot. Update affected task rows and targets instead of rebuilding the entire analysis by rote. Report when more evidence is truly necessary and why.

Simplicity means the least implementation burden that meets the ambitious objective, not the least hardware regardless of performance. A modest addition that substantially increases core throughput or secures an independently achievable RP may be an excellent simplification of the overall competitive problem. Conversely, adding mechanisms that do not improve ranking independence, expected wins or elite playoff output needs a strong opportunity-cost justification.
