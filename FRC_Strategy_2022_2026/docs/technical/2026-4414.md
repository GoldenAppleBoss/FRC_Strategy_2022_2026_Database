# 2026-4414: technical mechanisms, design reasoning and revision boundaries

These notes distinguish published construction from team-stated intent and original engineering inference. Selected numerical values are reported source data, not independently reproduced measurements. The recommended tests have not been performed. A binder snapshot does not certify every competition version.

## RIPCURRENT: shape the chassis and bumpers around the feed system

Version: 4414 RIPCURRENT interactive technical binder. [Technical source](https://2026.team4414.com/)

**Published construction:** Chamfered 25×32-inch base; offset battery rail; structural bumper/hopper walls and low electronics.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Preserve low rotor diameter and storage area within the perimeter.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** A singulator occupies a swept area, not just a rectangular bounding box. Changing chassis corners can preserve the parts of that area that most improve ring diameter while removing less useful perimeter. Moving the battery to an edge creates a new structural problem because the rail still has to carry drive and impact loads. Using the bumper backing as a hopper boundary can reclaim interior width, while tying scorer alignment to a removable impact structure. The package needs a repeatable bumper interface, and the stored-fuel center of mass must be evaluated alongside the empty robot.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Reinstall bumpers and measure scorer alignment; test loaded lateral acceleration and rotor clearance after representative impacts.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## RIPCURRENT: independently driven rotor, packing relief and passive contact

Version: 4414 RIPCURRENT interactive technical binder. [Technical source](https://2026.team4414.com/)

**Published construction:** Separate rotor/feed drives; shaped fin and wrap guides; passive rollers and omni contacts.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Reduce packing pressure, friction and belt slip.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** A bulk pile can load the singulator even when the exit path is clear. Agitating the pile and supplying the launch stream are different motions, so separating drives gives useful tuning freedom. A shaped fin can alter where that pile pressure is released; passive rollers reduce sliding resistance where balls push against each other. These details explain why a DYE-inspired design cannot be generalized as a generic circular indexer. The critical behavior is the changing network of contacts at full, partial and nearly empty loads, including a controlled reverse to clear a bridge.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Measure stream continuity and current at several load states; observe whether agitation increases entry occupancy or merely recirculates fuel.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## RIPCURRENT: compliant racks and a separately latched lower contact

Version: 4414 RIPCURRENT interactive technical binder. [Technical source](https://2026.team4414.com/)

**Published construction:** Stacked polycarbonate racks; primary and lower powered rollers; strengthened hubs and shear interfaces.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Impact compliance and reliable pickup with retained storage.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** Stacking plates can create controlled flex only if the plates and fasteners permit the intended relative motion. A low roller that remains deployed adds another ownership contact near the bumper, while its latch removes continuous holding load from an actuator. Stronger hubs and distributed shear interfaces address local failure modes that a thicker arm alone may not fix. The collector also moves storage boundaries, so its success must be measured while the hopper is loaded and the side walls are being pushed by fuel.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Test diagonal wall contact, rack synchronization and lower-latch retention; inspect roller-end alignment and capacity after impact.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## RIPCURRENT: compact shooter swept volume and stored energy

Version: 4414 RIPCURRENT interactive technical binder. [Technical source](https://2026.team4414.com/)

**Published construction:** Four-X44 3-inch flywheel; added dense inertia; powered hood roller; vertically stacked motor package.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Reduce protrusions while preserving the desired launch contact.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** A compact turret can improve the whole robot by leaving more feed and storage volume around its swept envelope. That benefit must be weighed against launch-contact repeatability, motor cooling, wire access and flywheel recovery. Dense inertia stores energy in limited space, but increases spin-up energy and bearing loads. A powered hood contact changes the spin state and must remain geometrically consistent as the hood moves. The key comparison with a drum is delivered scored fuel during usable windows, including time moving and acquiring, rather than a single instantaneous output rate.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Map exit velocity and spin versus hood angle, turret heading and volley length; inspect belt tracking under acceleration.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## RIPCURRENT: calibration and uncertainty remain empirical

Version: 4414 RIPCURRENT interactive technical binder. [Technical source](https://2026.team4414.com/) · [Team explanation](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/519602/116)

**Published construction:** Team reply describes robust-shot selection followed by measured angle and velocity calibration.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Choose trajectories tolerant of exit-state errors.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** A nominal ballistic solution is not enough when fuel compression, wear and launch contact change the actual exit velocity. Searching for a region of successful trajectories can improve tolerance, but the model must be anchored to measurements of the real mechanism. Separate localization, hood-reference and exit-speed errors before retuning: otherwise one correction can conceal another. This is a transferable technical procedure, not evidence that every team should adopt the same equation set or that the robot can score under every motion and defense condition.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Use stationary known-pose tests to isolate launch error, then add translation, rotation and tilt one at a time.
