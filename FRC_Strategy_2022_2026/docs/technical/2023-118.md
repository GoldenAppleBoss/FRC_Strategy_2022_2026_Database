## ECHO: compliant vector pickup and cone centering

Version: ECHO. [PDF p8](https://773cbb55-5509-49bc-8d49-05aa4e1c4c70.filesusr.com/ugd/209e0c_40103d44bfcb4b2b877c4b14bb9d1563.pdf#page=8)

**Published construction:** Sponge/noodle roller, dustpan, powered centering flappers and bent-polycarbonate structure.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** The binder lists construction; detailed comparative selection reasoning is not supplied.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** A broad soft contact can accommodate variation in approach and distribute pressure on a deformable piece. The dustpan supplies support underneath, while the flappers create a separate centering action. That is a different mechanism from merely adding compliant wheels to an arm. The soft material’s wear and compression set can alter intake behavior over an event; the structure must remain aligned while yielding to impacts. Because the binder does not give a complete rejected-alternatives account, these are engineering implications rather than a claim about the team’s exact selection process.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Measure centering success across cone orientations and roller wear, and inspect whether the dustpan or flappers create an escape path.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## ECHO: lightweight telescoping arm and roller end effector

Version: ECHO. [PDF p7](https://773cbb55-5509-49bc-8d49-05aa4e1c4c70.filesusr.com/ugd/209e0c_40103d44bfcb4b2b877c4b14bb9d1563.pdf#page=7) · [PDF p10](https://773cbb55-5509-49bc-8d49-05aa4e1c4c70.filesusr.com/ugd/209e0c_40103d44bfcb4b2b877c4b14bb9d1563.pdf#page=10)

**Published construction:** Sheet-metal pivot structure, carbon telescope, shimmed hex interfaces and light dead-axle end rollers.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Weight and backlash reduction are explicitly documented.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** Reducing distal mass improves both arm acceleration and the chassis stability needed to exploit it. A light telescoping tube still needs sufficient overlap and guidance to resist bending and torsion at full extension. Shimmed interfaces target lost motion, while structure and belt elasticity remain separate error sources. Dead-axle rollers can remove rotating shaft mass and simplify the wheel assembly, but their supports must retain alignment. A recommendation should compare endpoint settling and piece ownership under load rather than claiming a material alone makes the arm fast.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Measure endpoint deflection at full reach and after reversing both axes; inspect telescoping guidance and roller support wear.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## ECHO: nonparallel balance-fork linkage

Version: ECHO. [PDF p9](https://773cbb55-5509-49bc-8d49-05aa4e1c4c70.filesusr.com/ugd/209e0c_40103d44bfcb4b2b877c4b14bb9d1563.pdf#page=9)

**Published construction:** Carbon-rod forks, nonparallel four-bar lift and over-center deployment with ratchet release.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Lift and tilt relative to a partner and the floor.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** A nonparallel four-bar controls orientation as well as translation. That can lift a robot while changing its attitude without another actuator, but the force and mechanical advantage vary through the stroke. Over-center deployment can hold a state mechanically, while the ratchet provides a separate release/retention function. Partner geometry and platform angle determine the contact loads. The design should be retrieved as an alliance-specific balance solution rather than grouped indiscriminately with climbers from other seasons.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Model linkage force through the entire stroke and test with multiple partner undersides and off-axis loading.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## ECHO: preload catapult and vacuum swiper are distinct tactical tools

Version: ECHO. [PDF p11](https://773cbb55-5509-49bc-8d49-05aa4e1c4c70.filesusr.com/ugd/209e0c_40103d44bfcb4b2b877c4b14bb9d1563.pdf#page=11) · [PDF p12](https://773cbb55-5509-49bc-8d49-05aa4e1c4c70.filesusr.com/ugd/209e0c_40103d44bfcb4b2b877c4b14bb9d1563.pdf#page=12)

**Published construction:** Spring-released one-shot cube catapult; dual vacuum cups added in a three-day development period.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Preload launch and opponent loading-zone cube acquisition.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** A one-shot mechanism can be mechanically simpler than forcing the main manipulator to execute the same autonomous action, but it spends mass on a narrow use. Its value depends on repeatability and the time it frees for the rest of the routine. Vacuum acquisition relies on seal area, surface condition and resistance to peeling; it is poorly described by a generic grip-force number. The swiper is a dated tactical addition, not proof every earlier robot revision carried it. Keep the development date and task boundary attached to the feature.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Test preload release over the allowed setup variation and vacuum retention under peeling loads and imperfect cube surfaces.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.
