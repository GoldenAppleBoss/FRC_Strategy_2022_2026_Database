# Research complete architectures with minimal owner explanation

The owner should supply priorities and correct consequential mistakes. The research agent owns discovery, source extraction, reconstruction and engineering explanation. Do not require Jack to narrate every robot before research becomes possible. The five initial architecture cases show the expected depth and the attribution boundaries; they are examples, not proof that a particular topology wins every game.

## Objective and architecture selection

Design an elite primary contributor capable of independently achieving important ranking-point requirements and owning essential scoring prerequisites. Preserve the no-turret, no-pneumatics and no-support-only constraints. Simple and effective is the objective: fewer unnecessary independent motions, possession transfers and integration dependencies while retaining the capability that wins the game. A low motor count is not a performance target by itself. This doctrine does not lower the goal to a cheap or limited robot.

First identify the useful task set, route and throughput requirement from the actual game. Compare complete viable architectures under the same tasks, resources, rules and event assumptions. Only then optimize individual mechanisms. A brilliantly executed subsystem cannot rescue an architecture that spends its effort on the wrong job. Do not treat this statement as proof that any particular historical turret caused a poor season.

## Work the agent must perform

1. Discover candidates using the retained elite-performance cohort, official results and primary construction sources. Start with top performers but also include documented rebuilds, contrasting topologies and mechanically relevant out-of-cohort examples. A rank threshold is a discovery filter, not a causal design label. Do not expand a database by adding thin summaries when the missing information is possession, packaging or version history.
2. Search team build threads, interview captions, CAD releases, code and technical documents. Read the relevant source, not just a search snippet. Use selected code to test a control-sequence claim and exact source figures for visible geometry. Record commit, page, post or timestamp. A caption transcription is not a visual audit; a CAD link is not inspected CAD. Automatic captions require caution with numbers and names.
3. Reconstruct each complete path in plain language: where the piece starts, which surface first contacts it, what retains it, every possession transfer, the transit pose, scoring release and recovery. Separate distinct cone/cube paths or preload paths. Do not invent a transfer to make a diagram complete.
4. Write the engineering explanation yourself. It need not appear verbatim in a team publication. Anchor the premises, label the causal argument as inference, explain the physical mechanism and state when it would fail. Distinguish published intent, owner testimony, published outcome, analytical deduction and an unperformed test. Unknown intent is not a reason to omit sound engineering analysis.
5. Challenge the preferred architecture. State its strongest rival, useful capability that the rival buys, hidden costs in the simpler option and the conditions that reverse the choice. Preserve counterevidence such as complex robots with good service records. Avoid a popularity contest or a complexity morality tale.
6. Update canonical records, regenerate all derived views and run preservation, retrieval and attribution checks. A source being searchable does not prove the mechanical conclusion. Keep dated configuration boundaries; an IRI feature is not automatically a Worlds feature.

## Compare a vector of costs

Record independently controlled positioning coordinates, powered transport functions, binary actuations, possession handoffs, acquisition alignment burden, collision/control states, fabrication precision, service access, electrical demand and loaded dynamics separately. Stage count is not independent DOF count. Passive or mechanically coupled links may have one coordinate; roller motors still add power and control demands. Drive yaw and driver positioning can carry complexity removed from the mechanism. Do not sum these categories into an invented universal simplicity score.

For every additional motion or separate intake, answer: which essential task becomes possible or faster, how much complete-cycle improvement is plausible, what new transfer/clearance/control/service cost appears, and can passive geometry or chassis positioning achieve the same useful result? An added release mechanism may be worthwhile; a nominally simpler narrow intake may lose badly if approach time dominates.

## Worked analytical deduction: inclined fixed lift

Conditional on the owner's approximately 30–45 degree angle being measured above horizontal, a carriage moving distance s along the mast follows x = x0 + s cos(theta), z = z0 + s sin(theta). Therefore horizontal advance per vertical rise is cot(theta), about 1 to 1.73 over that angle range. This is an original geometric deduction from an attributed assumption, not a measurement of 1746. It immediately asks whether floor pickup, required scoring reach, legal extension and transit poses fit the same line. Add the hand/wrist reach and swept volume before claiming feasibility. If the angle convention differs, revise the model.

A fixed mast removes a shoulder coordinate but couples height and reach. Gravity along the rail is m g sin(theta); rail-normal gravity loading is m g cos(theta), before dynamics and other forces. These terms explain why return and bearing loading deserve attention, but do not size the actual elevator. A long stroke may require additional stages, reducing overlap or stiffness. Use real masses, stroke, rigging and friction before sizing motors or declaring an inertia advantage. Neither the owner account nor these equations establishes a measured cycle time.

## Complete-cycle and outcome evidence

Use Tcycle = Tacquire + Ttravel + Ttransfer + Tscore + Trecovery with overlapping operations handled explicitly. For approximately repeatable independent attempts, expected acquisition effort can scale like Tattempt / psuccess; this is a conditional model, not a season statistic. Do not invent p, ignore recovery travel or add simultaneous intervals twice. Compare distributions and failure modes, not just the cleanest clip. Include loaded driving and alignment in the scenario.

An Einstein appearance establishes competitive relevance. An alliance victory does not establish that one robot's architecture beat another architecture, and a season EPA estimate is not measured individual output. Keep the owner's claim that vision could improve 1746 as a hypothesis; estimate a gain only after measuring alignment/auto losses and demonstrating the proposed control change.

## Ask the owner only when the answer changes the work

Before escalating, inspect the available primary sources and separate configurations. If a missing dimension prevents exact CAD but not architecture comparison, preserve it as unknown and continue. Ask a narrow question when two plausible interpretations change the recommendation or when only the owner can resolve a consequential installation detail. Explain the decision affected; do not ask for an essay or for the owner to do candidate discovery. Stop dependent work while a genuinely necessary question is pending, and continue unrelated authorized research.

Current bounded questions: exact 1746 stage convention and angle, DCMP/Worlds/IRI feature chronology, and individual acquisition/alignment losses. None prevents the present source-bounded architecture studies. The 359/1833 comparison remains an attributed lead until mechanism, game-demand and performance evidence is gathered. No new claim about 359's construction or causal inferiority is established here.

## Acceptance before calling a case useful

A case needs a source/version boundary, typed premises, full supported piece path, package/swept-space explanation, independent motion inventory, avoided and retained costs, causal hypothesis, rival explanation, reversal condition, unresolved gaps and a decision-changing check. An image must be useful for its claimed scope; a thumbnail cannot certify hidden rigging. Missing imagery stays an explicit gap. No exact dimensions, performance rates or fabrication approval can be inferred from fame, captions or schematic drawings.

Machine checks enforce record completeness, parity, links and preservation. They do not validate the hypothesis or predict a future agent's design quality. Use the architecture evaluation cases to audit generated reasoning; human mechanical review and targeted physical evidence still decide whether an actual design is sound.
