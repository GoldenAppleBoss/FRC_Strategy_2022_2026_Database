# Explain a physical robot in plain language

Authority: explicit owner correction, 2026-10-27. Applies to every new recommendation, mechanism discussion and design review for 1833. This supplements the design doctrine and review protocol. It does not certify or rewrite historical robot evidence. The owner wants substantial context and technical depth; simple language does not mean short, shallow explanations.

## The reader must be able to picture the machine

After reading the recommendation, a teammate should be able to sketch the robot, describe how each piece moves from acquisition to release, and explain why the arrangement suits the game. Naming components, listing famous teams, citing sources or adding engineering vocabulary does not establish a design. Describe the physical robot before introducing shorthand. Define a specialized term at its first use and attach it to a specific component and action. Use familiar FRC terms where helpful; avoid repeated definitions once the meaning is clear.

For example, replace “moment-reacting guidance” with “bearings above and below the carriage keep it from twisting when the loaded gripper hangs in front of the mast.” Then explain which surfaces the bearings contact and how their spacing is chosen. Replace “positive retention” with the proposed physical action: “a hinged keeper closes over the pipe so it cannot bounce out of the two lower saddles.” Explain what carries the load, how the keeper opens and what happens without power. These are illustrative explanations, not approved mechanisms for any particular game.

Do not fix jargon by deleting substance. Expand the explanation until the geometry, force path and operating sequence are understandable. Keep the useful technical term afterward if it improves communication. Do not force a word-count limit or compress nuance into a checklist.

## Explain every major mechanism using these six questions

1. Job and complete sequence: What does it accomplish? Describe approach, first contact, capture, retained orientation, any handoff, scoring and return to the next acquisition. Name each moving part and what drives it. Identify how the driver or controller knows a step succeeded. If there is no storage or handoff, say so and explain the direct path.
2. Physical construction: Describe the actual proposed rollers, plates, shafts, bearings, belts, chains, guides and actuators as relevant. Explain what supports what and which structure carries gravity, acceleration and contact loads. Separate a preliminary mechanism family from a specified build; do not invent ratios or tolerances for apparent completeness.
3. Historical inspiration: Identify the exact year, team, version, source locator and inspected figure. Describe the source mechanism in ordinary language first. Identify exactly which feature survives in the new design, what changes and what is original. Explain why the retained feature solves this interface. If only a generic principle survives, call it that rather than presenting the new mechanism as a replica or a proven transfer. Elite EPA is not mechanism validation. There is no quota of famous robot references.
4. Packaging: Explain its location, orientation and attachment on the chassis; approximate occupied space; start, acquisition, carry, scoring and recovery positions; and the space swept during motion. Explain nearby wheels, bumpers, battery, electronics, other mechanisms and service access. State which dimensions are manual/CAD facts, source measurements or provisional allocations. Account for the held piece as well as robot structure. Trace the difficult transition, not just a box labeled “intake” beside a box labeled “elevator.”
5. Choice: Explain the strongest practical alternative and why this arrangement is worth its mass, space, power, manufacturing, control and service burden. Distinguish a team-stated historical reason from the agent's engineering explanation. Simple construction is a means to elite useful output, not permission for a low capability ceiling.
6. Engineering support and remaining limits: Identify which rule, dimension, load, calculation or evidence constrains the choice. Show how the result changes component spacing, reach, speed, capacity or the decision. Identify what would invalidate it and the cheapest adequate way to resolve that uncertainty. Link these facts to the physical explanation rather than dumping equations in an unrelated section.

## Whole-robot packaging is required

Describe the robot from front, side and top in prose and provide a labeled rough layout when spatial understanding needs it. A hand sketch, vector drawing, or simple diagram is sufficient; finished CAD is not required. A flowchart explains order, not occupied space, and cannot substitute for a physical layout. Drawings must label orientation, approximate scale or dimensions, factual versus assumed geometry, moving positions and important unresolved clearances. Images must actually resolve from the delivered output location. Use source crops with a locator and a sentence identifying the visible feature. Do not invent a dimension from an undimensioned photo or use generated imagery as geometric evidence.

State where heavy equipment sits and where each mechanism attaches. Follow one piece through every transition and show where interference could occur. Consider bumper crossings, ground reach, wheel steering sweep, scoring approach, cables through moving joints, cleaning jams, replacing belts, removing the battery and powered-off unloading. Do not require irrelevant interfaces: apply each check to the actual robot and game. Keep a single consistent arrangement across narrative, layout, calculations and any machine-readable records.

If a critical dimension is missing, identify the dimension, give a clearly labeled feasible range if one can be derived, explain which proposed arrangement fails outside it, and make the recommendation conditional. Do not fill the gap with invented precision. Unknown geometry does not excuse a vague arrangement; it changes how firmly the arrangement can be recommended. Expensive CAD and physical iteration should wait until cheaper checks resolve decision-changing uncertainty.

## Calculations must earn their place

For each consequential calculation explain the input provenance, units, relevant loading or operating condition, simplifications, result and design consequence. A piece-weight moment alone does not size a wrist: explain tool weight, bearing reactions, drive torque, holding, acceleration and unknown contact loads as relevant. A battery amp-hour calculation does not establish voltage sag. An arbitrary current allowance does not establish an actuator choice. Do not demand exact component sizing at architecture stage when evidence supports only a bound; explain that bound and its consequence.

Separate targets from predicted results and both from measured performance. A complete-cycle assumption is still an assumption even when its arithmetic is correct. Show ranges and break-even conditions where small changes reverse the architecture. Compare different strategic investments before refining two mechanisms with the same mission. If the common strategic choice is already justified, state the earlier evidence and scope the comparison accordingly. Include an opponent deliberately attacking the chosen robot's dependencies. For RP thresholds, distinguish a schedule that fits from a robust successful route; use illustrative probability calculations only with explicit assumptions about dependence, recovery and opponent actions.

## Delivery and review

Lead with the proposed robot and why it wins useful work. Follow with an understandable whole-robot tour and complete piece cycles, then evidence, alternatives and engineering support. Preserve long explanations where necessary. Source IDs and JSON fields support the explanation; they are not the explanation. A recommendation must stand on its own without requiring the reader to open an absent decision record for its central argument.

Before delivery, check: Can a teammate sketch the arrangement? Can they explain each actuator's action? Do they know which exact historical features were borrowed? Can they follow the piece through every transition? Do they know what calculations constrained the decision and what remains unknown? Revise any failed answer. Do not claim these human comprehension checks have been passed by a person unless they actually have.
