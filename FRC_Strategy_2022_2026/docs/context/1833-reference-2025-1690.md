## 1690, 2025 WHISPER: compact functional description, coupled mechanics and execution

Primary facts: Orbit credits a closed-belt differential elevator reference and describes floor pickup as a cycle-time choice. Team posts identify pressure feedback and maintenance of an adhesive belt connection. Its championship recap discusses alliance interference and switching a partner to predictable loading-station lanes. A later intake-assist post explains driver-intent conditions and velocity correction. These accounts establish that optimization includes control and traffic management, not only a short mechanism list. They do not show that a differential or vacuum is universally simpler for another team.

1833 transfer analysis: separating moving hardware from actuator location can reduce inertia while creating motor-space coupling and calibration work. A differential should be assessed over simultaneous lift/pivot demands, saturation and failure recovery rather than judged by the number of motors saved. A vacuum can make a gripper visually small while moving complexity into sealing, pressure generation, state sensing and maintenance. Those are valid choices if they improve the whole cycle under the team's resources; they are not free complexity elimination.

For match modeling, faster individual floor pickup does not necessarily produce faster alliance throughput if robots fight over the same accessible pieces or routes. Compare shared supply and traffic policies explicitly. A robot designed to dominate can adopt a lane or temporary complementary assignment without having been designed as a weak support machine. The historical alliance discussion is therefore useful to 1833's elite ceiling even though support-only architecture is prohibited. Reserve time to test driver assistance at speed with realistic errors; a theoretically optimal command that surprises the driver may degrade acquisition. Observe driver workload and repeatability as well as mechanism motion time.
