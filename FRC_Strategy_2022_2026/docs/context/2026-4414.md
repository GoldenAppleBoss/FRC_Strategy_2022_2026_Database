# 4414 in 2026: storage, a compact turret and an entire operating policy

RIPCURRENT is useful as an architecture case because its components reinforce each other. The low singulator, small turret swept volume, chassis shape, storage walls, net and movable collection system allocate the same scarce interior space. Merely writing “turret with a big hopper” loses the reason the package is interesting. The base dossier and the team’s interactive binder describe the construction; this supplement adds the team’s explanations of calibration, autonomy, geometry and iteration. [Interactive technical binder](https://2026.team4414.com/). [Discussion](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/519602).

## Preserve the terms before reasoning from them

The relevant singulator reference is DYE Rotor, a paintball-loader family. It should not be parsed as “di-rotor,” an assertion that there are two rotors, or a generic synonym for any spindexer. The architecture has to be identified from its actual feed paths and independent drives. Likewise a turret makes azimuth independent of chassis heading within its usable range; it does not by itself establish accuracy at every location, continuous firing under every acceleration or immunity to defense.

The current reviewed capacity claims in the binder are 85+ fuel in the trench-capable configuration and 100+ with the net over the bump. The user’s 110-fuel example remains unverified in this edition. A 110-inch frame-perimeter reference must not be turned into 110 stored fuel. An additional later measurement could resolve that question, but a persuasive narrative is not a substitute for it. Capacity also needs a defined condition: static fill, retained fuel while moving, fuel actually deliverable to the shooter and a particular net/deployment configuration can all differ.

## The geometry is a system-level choice

The base dossier records the compact turret, the large low rotor, chamfered chassis, offset battery packaging and structural storage walls. The team later describes an early inspiration that changed its thinking from a square machine toward a wider chamfered shape. This is evidence of design lineage, not a claim that the exact inspirational sketch was fabricated. [October reflection](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/519602/157).

A useful agent generalization is that reducing an assembly’s swept volume can be worth more than making its standalone performance better. A smaller turret can leave more storage around it, permit a better floor route and reduce the space needed for wiring. Those gains can increase how long the robot remains useful in a scoring shift. But the tradeoff must be tested: a compact shooter may have poorer compression consistency, less flywheel inertia, more difficult service or a tighter hood path. The geometry only wins if the integrated robot delivers more reliable output under the target conditions.

The published images include separate rotor V1–V10 and shooter V1–V11 asset labels. These are preserved in the gallery. Some filenames mention pre-Ventura, Ventura or Orange County; the other V-numbers should not be assigned to event configurations without further evidence. An image of a prototype is evidence that a design was considered or published, not that it survived qualification, played at Worlds or ran simultaneously with every other pictured part.

## Do not copy the successful calibration story without the empirical part

The team says its shot solver drew from 1690’s 2022 presentation, but searched a region of shots reaching the near and far rim rather than only targeting the center. It chose solutions with tolerance to exit-speed and angle error. The team then measured angle offset and empirically calibrated exit speed against commanded wheel speed; one shooter revision needed a nonlinear lookup. It frames the benefit as reducing tuning effort, while explicitly not claiming the season’s best shot or best shooting on the move. [Shot-model explanation](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/519602/116).

The independent engineering implication is to model uncertainty, not only nominal trajectories. Ball wear, contact state, feeder speed and wheel compression can change actual exit conditions. If an architecture relies on a computed trajectory but cannot measure or repeat the exit state, adding more aerodynamic terms may not solve its dominant error. Conversely, a well-designed mechanism can make a compact empirical calibration useful across many conditions. An agent should recommend an experiment that identifies the dominant uncertainty before recommending a more complicated simulator.

For a different shooter, the transferable goal is a maintainable calibration procedure. That procedure should say what changes when fuel ages, when a wheel is replaced, when the feed geometry is adjusted and when a hood reference drifts. It should also say how the team distinguishes a localization error from an exit-velocity error. Otherwise a driver can lose an entire event chasing the wrong tuning parameter.

## Vision hardware and the hardware that actually worked are different records

The team describes a turret-mounted Limelight using MegaTag 1 and supplementary USB cameras on an Orange Pi. Historical gyro and turret angles are buffered so camera observations can be transformed at their measurement time before filtering and fusion. The team reports no precise absolute turret zero, using a visually approximated starting zero and accepting a small consequence for the gyro reference. It calls that a local prioritization decision. [Vision account](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/519602/107).

The team separately says it ran Champs eliminations with only one working camera on the Orange Pi after USB-port failures, with ESD offered as a suspected explanation. This establishes the difference between intended sensor architecture and the functional configuration in an important competition period; it does not prove the electrical failure mechanism. [Functional configuration](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/519602/111).

This case should therefore not become an agent rule that absolute sensing is unnecessary or that any one camera platform is inherently unreliable. The useful lesson is that the target’s tolerance, the observation geometry and the team’s familiarity can change the value of a more rigorous reference system. A different game with narrow placement or multi-target tasks can expose errors that a large hub did not. An architecture recommendation needs both nominal performance and degraded operation when a camera, port or reference fails.

## A lower peak autonomous routine can be a better alliance routine

The team explains testing depot-auto variants and finding that double swipes left insufficient time to fire and reach the desired transition position. It preferred crossing the bump once to improve compatibility and avoid being affected if a partner got stuck. The chosen path also depended on a fuel triangle that did not reliably form without enough robots rushing midfield. [Autonomous tradeoff](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/519602/124).

That dependency should remain attached to the recommendation. A routine optimized for four strong midfield contestants may collect less at a sparse local event or with a different opponent plan. Similarly, a transition endpoint has value because of what the robot can do next, not because “center” is universally optimal. The agent should compare complete auto-plus-transition outcomes under several partner/opponent configurations, including an obstruction and a failed pickup. Scoring more in the first interval can lose more in the next interval if the machine ends in a poor location with an empty hopper.

## Collision recovery is part of useful throughput

The team reports changing the bottom plastic from 1/16 to 1/8 inch after weight became available, and finding that the stiffer sheet helped the robot slide off fuel. The same reply describes an unsuccessful camera-wiring experiment that damaged hardware. Both belong in the record, but neither should be copied as a generic recipe. [Packaging and belly-sheet account](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/519602/105).

This matters strategically because a robot with lower nominal firing rate can outperform a faster one if it spends less time stranded, realigning or hunting for a feedable pile. The correct comparison is fuel successfully scored during useful windows, including the time needed to acquire, transfer, aim, recover and return. It should be measured against representative defense and fuel distribution. A claim that this turret “almost outscored the best drums” needs an identified match, the other robots’ roles and the relevant scoring conditions; this edition does not turn that phrase into a universal ranking of mechanism families.

## What a recommendation inspired by RIPCURRENT should contain

Recommend the package when independent aiming, mobile collection and storage preservation match the team’s intended role and development capacity. State the cost in singulator development, calibration, wiring, sensor recovery and service access. Compare it against a braced fixed shooter with a simpler floor and a wide drum under the same supply and defense assumptions. Prototype partially depleted and mixed-load feeding, not just a full static hopper. Keep a dated configuration record for every claimed improvement. The available evidence is strong enough to make RIPCURRENT a useful source of architectural ideas; it is not complete enough to certify every intermediate competition version, the user’s exact 110-fuel number or a guaranteed advantage against any named drum.
