## 1690, 2024 Doppler: compact primary scorer, substantial control development

Primary facts: the reveal gives an approximately 11-inch highest stowed point. Official results identify Orbit as a 2024 world champion. The mechanism is chassis-yaw aimed; its elevation/pivot motion is not a turret. In the CAD release, a team member warns that low packaging did not make every individual mechanism superior and that substantial work was needed. The team's controls explanation describes physically limited motion commands, skid/collision handling, driver assistance and automated shot readiness. Treat this as a team account rather than an independent performance decomposition.

1833 transfer analysis: low CG can increase usable acceleration and reduce settling, but free speed or stowed height alone cannot predict cycles. Compare time to accelerate, brake, reacquire heading, capture a piece and release a valid shot under contact. The cost of chassis aiming must be included in a non-turret architecture's state model. It may be small when acquisition exits with a useful heading and the driver can approach efficiently; it may be large when position and heading are constrained together. Geometry and software must be co-designed so that the driver requests remain physically feasible.

This example validates ambition with a compact architecture. It does not justify eliminating a necessary game function, assuming development is easy, or concluding that the lowest robot always wins. A low package that creates rare disabling piece poses may lose more match contribution than it gains in an ideal cycle. Preserve the existing note-deflector study as a reliability problem alongside the positive architecture example. The future design should carry an explicit optimization budget: time for loaded tuning, autonomous coordination, repeated defended acquisition, operator feedback and service drills. Hardware simplicity buys development capacity only if the saved effort is actually invested in those areas.
