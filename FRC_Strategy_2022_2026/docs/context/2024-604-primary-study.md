# 2024-604 — Quixilver: Domotron

Primary source: https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/team-604-quixilver-presents-our-2024-robot-cad-and-code-release/477799

Reviewed 2026-10-26. This is a source-bounded technical study. Construction, stated intent, interpretation and unresolved evidence are separate.

## 2024 release and May 2025 follow-up · intake

### Published construction or code

Polycarbonate rollers flex under impact; short dead-axle sections avoid a full-width shaft permanently bending. The team reports rollers deflecting more than half their diameter in bad hits. Deployment ties the two sides through chain and a cross shaft inside the frame.

### Stated rationale and boundaries

Compliance, weight and packaging drove the split-axle construction. The team explicitly reports axle-mount bolts shearing and deployment chain snapping, unresolved during the season.

### Engineering interpretation

A compliant roller is not the same as a robust entire intake. Moving deformation away from a long shaft can concentrate load in axle bolts or a mechanically coupled deployment system. This matters directly to 1833’s request for something beefy: copy the load path and its known limitations, not merely the visible material. The 2026 independently driven arms are a later response to the chain failure, not a configuration that existed throughout 2024.

### Validation still required

Verify the installed configuration and interfaces against the original publication/CAD; prototype full acquisition-to-output cycles, recovery and service access under the intended loaded and defended conditions. These tests are proposed, not performed.

## 2024 published CAD versus installed hardware · controls

### Published construction or code

The team says bellypan holes were hand-drilled and omitted from CAD, electronics were suppressed to reduce CAD lag, shooter shafts were actually cut shorter, and a reused climber shaft tensioned surgical tubing absent from CAD.

### Stated rationale and boundaries

Some differences were drafting omissions, some deliberate reuse and some effort-versus-benefit decisions.

### Engineering interpretation

A released CAD assembly is a valuable geometry reference but not a perfect as-built record. A long shaft in a rendering is not automatically an intended design feature. Likewise, suppressed electronics do not imply an unplanned electrical layout. Follow the team’s replies when interpreting the release. The existing interview synopsis documents the asymmetric launcher and trap system; this addition focuses on the explicitly published manufacturing and intake corrections.
