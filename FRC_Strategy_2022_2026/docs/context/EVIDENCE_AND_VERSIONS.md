# Evidence, versions and what “reviewed” means

This edition adds long contextual notes, technical images, a RoboReplay review ledger and post-level research locators. These additions deliberately preserve the original coverage gaps. A longer account is not an exhaustive account, and a larger bibliography is not evidence that every item was read.

## Keep several timelines

A publication date, design revision date, competition use date and interview recording date can differ. An offseason release can describe an onseason part, a removed prototype or a new offseason modification. A CAD V-number can be a convenient export label rather than a competition configuration. Store those dates separately and leave them null when not established. Do not use a forum post date as the installation date of a mechanism.

A robot version should name the relevant assembly changes, the evidence supporting them and the interval where it is known to apply. If the interval is unknown, retain the change without inventing a date. Do not merge every described feature into a single ideal robot. The 1768, 1323 and 4414 deep notes include examples where doing so would create misleading architecture and performance claims.

## Distinguish the kinds of statements

- Official rules establish requirements for their dated version; they do not establish what a team built.
- A team release establishes what the publisher reports, with the conditions and uncertainty it provides.
- A reported measurement needs configuration, fuel/piece condition, metric definition and test setting.
- A commentator can identify a useful interpretation, but observed motion does not reveal private intent or prove a failure diagnosis.
- Analyst reasoning should expose its assumptions and the experiment that could reject it.
- A discovery locator points to something worth reading; it is not a summarized technical claim.

An agent should retrieve evidence at the level of the claim. A post supporting a transmission change does not establish the climber. A video discussing finals does not visually audit every match. An official phase-score table can locate where points differed without explaining why they differed.

## Caption review is a specific access method

All 33 downloaded RoboReplay transcripts were reviewed through their available text, including recovery of portions truncated in intermediate output. No claim is made to have visually watched their diagrams, replay frames or uncaptioned details. Automatic captions often substitute numbers, confuse “not” and “now,” or distort mechanism names. Exact scores and team identities should be cross-checked with the linked event record. The review notes therefore preserve uncertainty rather than copying questionable ASR numbers.

The ledger contains 44 discovered video IDs, sourced chiefly from the channel author’s announcements in two Chief Delphi discussions plus the season recap. Eleven discovered videos remain unreviewed after a transcript request was blocked. Direct channel enumeration was not completed, so 44 is a discovered lower bound, not the certified channel size. No access-control workaround was used. Future authorized access can update the ledger and this status without rewriting already reviewed notes.

The distributed package contains original summaries, analysis, metadata and timestamp navigation labels. It does not redistribute complete captions or entire forum discussions. This keeps the reference useful without turning it into a bulk copy of other creators’ work. Follow the original links for their complete explanations, diagrams and demonstrations.

## Image labels carry their own uncertainty

The first extraction produced 318 source-associated images. Curation removed non-mechanism covers, logos, screenshots, decorative material and several comparison images whose depicted team could not be established. Named team-published iteration images were added, and PDF figures were reconstructed with their transparency masks where available. The final manifest is authoritative for included counts.

A contact-sheet review establishes that an image is a useful technical figure or robot view; it does not certify every detail or dimension. A binder page’s keywords describe the surrounding discussion and may not label that particular figure. A named team asset is a stronger component label, but still does not prove competition use. Each asset therefore retains its original source, page or post, date when available, publisher label and review basis. Attribution is not a new license; consult the source before other reuse.

## When sources conflict

Retain both claims with their dates and evidence classes. Try to resolve whether they describe different versions, test conditions, definitions or simple errors. Do not average incompatible numbers. Do not prefer a newer commentary clip over a direct team explanation merely because it sounds confident. The unresolved 110-fuel claim and the disputed 2022 possession interpretation are deliberately visible examples.

For architecture recommendations, a conflict may not need immediate resolution if the decision is robust across the plausible values. State that explicitly. If it changes the selection—for example, whether capacity is sufficient for the planned active window—make it a required measurement before committing. This is more useful than hiding the uncertainty behind a single polished number.
