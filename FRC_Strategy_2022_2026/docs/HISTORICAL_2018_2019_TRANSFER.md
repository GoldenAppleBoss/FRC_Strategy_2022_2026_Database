# Using the 2018 and 2019 expansion

This expansion adds two game chapters and thirteen curated robot-year studies: 2018 teams 148, 254, 971, 1610, 1678, 2898 and 2910; 2019 teams 254, 971, 1323, 1610, 1678 and 2910. They were selected for primary technical evidence and useful contrasts, not by a newly retrieved EPA ranking. The original 2022–2026 selections remain intact. A curated historical expansion is not an additional fifty-team top-25 census, and no robot has an exhaustive every-version certification.

The studies combine technical binders, workshop slides, team explanations and a pinned 1323 code snapshot. Slides were read as slides, including selected figures; no narrated workshop or reveal-video watching is implied. Formal binders and first-party discussion have separate review scopes. Missing geometry, unknown event installation and unpublished selection rationale remain explicit. The agent can retrieve these records from the same packet, claim, image and SQLite interfaces as the later seasons.

## Start with the new game's differences

The question is not “which old game does this look like?” Write the new task/state graph before looking for mechanisms. Does possession allow one piece or several? Does value accrue per item, by target occupancy, or through seconds of control? Can an opponent remove value, deny access, or merely slow transport? Does a bonus require a quantity, several different heights, a particular ordered sequence, autonomous behavior, or multiple physical robots? Which requirements can 1833 directly own?

Then query by physical function. A 2018 box intake may offer useful contact geometry for an irregular new box. A 2019 handoff may offer a way to reduce elevator payload. A 2023 cone/cube wrist may offer a useful multi-pose workspace. These transfers are hypotheses to compare, not an instruction to select the most famous old robot. A new manual can share mechanisms with several seasons while demanding a different architecture and match strategy.

## How to read “why it worked” responsibly

Every mechanism note separates construction, a team-stated reason or explicit lack of one, engineering interpretation, proposed validation and limitations. Do not convert the interpretation into an interview quote. Do not turn a proposed test into an experiment already performed. A speed in a binder remains a source-reported parameter until its timing endpoints, loads and repeatability are established.

For a mechanism to transfer, state the causal chain. For example: lowering carried intake mass can reduce arm torque; reduced torque can permit faster controlled motion or lower current; that helps match output only if acquisition/transfer and alignment do not become the new bottleneck. The first implication follows mechanics. The later implications require assumptions about the new layout and cycle. A list of three impressive team numbers does not establish a compatible robot.

For every borrowed idea identify input piece pose, output pose, available space, moving mass, compliance, actuator type, load path, sensors, failure state, service direction and partner/field interface. A servo, motor, spring, latch or gravity deployment is a redesign with different behavior, not a word substitution for a cylinder.

## Version distinctions the agent must retain

1. 2018 Citrus: the included earlier horizontal roller CAD is not the later 1323-derived intake. The team intentionally omitted that later geometry.
2. 2019 Citrus: the modeled elevator brake was never installed on the competition robot, according to the team reply.
3. 2019 254: short arm/larger regional intake/stinger versus longer lighter arm/compact intake/suction climb. Do not mix their load assumptions or imply the hybrid competed.
4. 2018 2898: scale-capable V1 versus switch/exchange V2. Retained intake/drive hardware does not mean retained reach or strategy.
5. 2018 971: the lift slide names V1/V2, but the reviewed slides do not establish exact event dates or a narrated comparison.
6. 2019 1323: tentative five-amp lift holding estimate, later two-amp report, and a misidentified 3310-2018 image in the discussion. The code snapshot is pinned but is not certified as the championship binary.
7. 2019 254's public offseason swerve repository is a different snapshot from the in-season West Coast drivetrain. Do not retrofit the offseason drive into championship performance claims.
8. 2019 2910's practice climb with 1323 does not make them a championship alliance. Keep practice, division and Einstein records separate.

## The two ranking lessons are different

2018 rewards ownership time and requires three robots' line crossings for AUTO QUEST. A primary robot can supply switch ownership but cannot directly make absent partners cross. A partner lift plus LEVITATE can own more of the climbing burden while still requiring a compatible partner to dock.

2019 allows one robot to supply all six panels and six cargo to a rocket if time, access and reliability suffice. But one level-3 climb is 12 points, below the 15-point HAB threshold. Distinguish solo piece-scoring capability from alliance endgame RP. An agent must identify the exact irreducible dependency instead of promising complete independence or abandoning the objective for a support role.

The common 1833 lesson is to own the highest-value attainable prerequisites, maximize reliable useful output and preserve qualification leverage. The mechanical embodiment is game-dependent. The 2026 fuel-throughput/climb priority should not be copied blindly into either historical game or a new manual.

## Evidence and open research

The expansion has detailed notes for selected mechanisms, rather than dimensions for every bolt or a claim to have watched every historical match. `data/historical_2018_2019_audit.json` identifies the curated cohort, review boundaries, source files/page counts and new image count. Parameter records retain source notation. Image crops retain source page and normalized bounds; they are mechanism references, not engineering drawings. A raw source or publication link still has its original rights and availability constraints.

No new 2018/2019 EPA ranking table, PCH week-three/week-five/DCMP performance thresholds, or per-match Einstein tactical reviews were created by this expansion. Existing later-season benchmarks are preserved and must not be relabeled as historical values. Source gaps should generate research requests or lower precision in the answer, not invented quantitative confidence.
