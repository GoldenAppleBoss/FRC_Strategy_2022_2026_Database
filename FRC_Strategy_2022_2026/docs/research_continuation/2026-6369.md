# 2026-6369: A drum rebuild shifts the problem to inertia, loading and inspection mass

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The interview distinguishes the former turret from a four-X60 drum rebuild. A long kicker ramp maintains contact; a stub-roller floor pivots with the collector, with a jackshaft inside a roller. Passive hopper/net expansion is reported as roughly 50–55 fuel unexpanded and up to 78 expanded. A 1.5-lb steel flywheel with a stated 1:3 transmission supplements launcher inertia. The planned L1 mechanism was removed at inspection.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team attributes the ramp to sustained compression, the added flywheel to speed droop while feeding a large load, and climb removal to a discrepancy between shop and event weighing. A revised hood ratio sought a compromise between actuation speed and resistance to disturbance.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

The supplementary flywheel stores energy in the motor-to-drum system; its contribution depends on the square of its speed ratio. That does not establish a sustained power budget, so a high first-volley rate may still decay. The pivoting floor improves access but introduces moving shaft/alignment interfaces. The source caption calls one small drum dimension ambiguously; do not infer diameter from that wording. Likewise, a removed climber is an unfielded feature for the filmed configuration, not an autonomous capability.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Measure speed droop over a complete hopper and repeated shifts, verify drive/driven ratio order, and calibrate scales before allocating the final mass margin.
