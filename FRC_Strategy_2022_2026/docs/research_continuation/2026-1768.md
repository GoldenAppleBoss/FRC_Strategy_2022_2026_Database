# 2026-1768: Rebuild removes feed compression and separates shooter readiness

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The interview distinguishes a former divided two-lane/double-spindexer arrangement from the drum rebuild with a roller floor. A powered lower intake roller was replaced by two 3/4-inch aluminum shafts with reduced compression. Compliant wheels near entry feed a J-shaped path; a guide was added after WPI wobble. Four motors drive the drum/kicker/hood group, and feed is enabled only after aiming, speed and hood conditions are satisfied.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team reports improved throughput after reducing collector compression, adds the guide to control observed motion, and describes reduced belt tension as a current-draw improvement. Driver controls were intentionally simplified around repeatable actions.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

This is evidence against treating maximum grip as maximum throughput. Too many opposing powered contacts can trap deformable fuel or make its entry velocity inconsistent. Removing a roller changes the entire force chain, so the improvement should not be generalized to a collector with different ground geometry. Likewise, the software shoot command is a sequencer rather than a simultaneous motor-on command. Feeding early can pull down wheel speed or create an inaccurate initial volley, while waiting unnecessarily long reduces usable shift time.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Compare feed latency, initial-volley accuracy, current and residual fuel with the actual readiness tolerances. Inspect the added guide over hood travel and repeat reduced-compression tests with worn fuel.
