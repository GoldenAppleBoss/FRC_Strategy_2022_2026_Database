## Published / retained construction account

The collector deploy angle was flattened after first-event jams. The retained interview account describes surgical tubing replaced by grippier floor surfaces after fuel debris caused slip. The shooter uses a four-X60 aluminum drum, powered upper rollers and a compact geared steel flywheel; transfer is sequenced after launcher speed is reached. Current, voltage and temperature logging inform operating limits.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team links the added flywheel to reduced speed decay and improved end-of-hopper accuracy. Floor/collector changes respond to observed jamming and contamination, while software limiting responds to the total electrical demand.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

A material choice can work with clean practice fuel yet fail when loose surface debris changes friction. A flywheel can smooth a transient without fixing an inadequate recharge rate or a hot motor. The useful design reference is therefore a tested material/contact path plus an explicit electrical budget, not a motor count. Robot logging should distinguish commanded voltage, actual bus voltage, current limit, speed error and fuel flow; otherwise starvation and electrical saturation can look like the same shooting symptom.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Repeat full-hopper tests with used fuel and low batteries. Compare surface cleanliness, slip, speed recovery and motor temperature over multiple shifts rather than a single successful volley.
