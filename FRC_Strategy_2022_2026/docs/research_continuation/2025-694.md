# 2025-694: A dedicated low-task arm adds work without monopolizing the elevator

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The station funnel uses powered surfaces and an IR entry sensor. The separate Froggy mechanism acquires coral/algae from the floor and scores L1/processor; its source gives a 48:1 X60 pivot and 2:1 X60 roller drive. A linked shaft/chain keeps the side plates synchronized, while added angle reinforcement stiffens their weak direction.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team describes reinforcement after structural analysis and a software interlock that delays retracting Froggy until the chassis backs away from the reef. The main arm’s motor placement is also used for counterbalance.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

Low-level work can justify a dedicated mechanism when it shortens cycles and releases the primary scorer for another role. That benefit is conditional on interference: two mechanisms must be able to store, acquire and move without colliding. A geometric safety interlock should therefore be part of the architecture reference. “Button released” is not sufficient evidence that an external mechanism can retract safely.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Test concurrent main-arm/Froggy poses, compare complete L1/algae cycles, and verify retreat detection before retraction under localization error.
