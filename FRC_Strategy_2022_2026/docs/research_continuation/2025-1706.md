# 2025-1706: Cable control and a later algae arm are essential version details

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The robot retains an elevator-based station-fed scorer and later adds a compact curved-rack algae intake. A constant-force spring pulls the elevator cable carrier inward after it repeatedly broke, flopped outward or entered the funnel. The climber uses the funnel’s rear region and was revised to grab one cage bar.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team describes the algae arm as lightweight and quick to deploy, and identifies the spring as the response to cable-carrier failures. The updated climb seeks easier capture rather than a larger nominal grasp.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

A cable carrier is a moving structural interface, not an afterthought in the electrical system. Its force, bend radius and free envelope affect elevator current and what can enter the station funnel. An added algae collector also changes the safe pose space of an already working scorer. The architecture should retain the dated add-on history rather than imply the final robot existed at the first event.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Run full-stroke cable fatigue tests and check the spring at maximum extension. Test coral loading with the algae mechanism deployed and verify cage capture under angular misalignment.

Focused technical passages and the retained dossier are the evidence scope. Exact event installation, complete CAD tolerances and all intermediate revisions are not certified.
