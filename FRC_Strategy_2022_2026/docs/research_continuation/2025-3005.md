# 2025-3005: A serializer rebuild must eliminate stable wrong orientations

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

Before Worlds, passive polycarbonate funneling and one lower belt were replaced by molded powered flaps that contact awkward coral orientations. The retained ladder-style placement mechanism also received a cam after early L4 tipping/alignment difficulties. The interview separately describes a ratcheting climb that can retain partial progress after disable.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The serializer change is explicitly tied to coral settling into specific trapped orientations. Retaining a climb after power-off is described as an important requirement, not an incidental effect of gearing.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

A funnel that succeeds in the common orientation can still create a stable mechanical equilibrium in an uncommon one. Powered flaps need to disturb that equilibrium without ejecting or crushing the piece. Likewise, an L4 scorer should be judged by placement stability and alignment delay, not only reaching the nominal height. These are distinct revisions and should not be attributed to one unchanging opening-week robot.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Deliberately place coral in every previously trapped pose, measure recovery time, and test L4 placement after abrupt chassis stops. Verify retention with the actual disabled climb configuration.

## Limits

Focused technical passages and the retained dossier are the evidence scope. Exact event installation, complete CAD tolerances and all intermediate revisions are not certified.
