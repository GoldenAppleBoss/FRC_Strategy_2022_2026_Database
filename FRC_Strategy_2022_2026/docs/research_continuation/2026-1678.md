# 2026-1678: Protected carbon intake and continuous J-path launcher feed

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

A 2×1 aluminum crash member protects a carbon intake roller. Later wrist plates use half-inch stock with raised hard-stop contact regions and pocketing. The shooter uses a J-shaped accelerating tunnel, a separate feeder, a four-X60 steel drum and powered hood rollers; its hood pivot is supported independently of the drum. Two side cameras were added to the original front Limelight.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team links the crash member to allowing a light but impact-sensitive roller, larger hard-stop contact to reduced wear, low electronics/wiring to a lower floor, and additional cameras to midfield vision gaps found at Half Moon Bay. The source also describes replacing an earlier S-shaped path after direction reversal hurt consistency.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

The useful package combines structural load routing with flow continuity. The crash member must intercept contact before the carbon roller does; copying only the carbon tube removes the condition that made it practical. A lower floor reduces the climb imposed on loose fuel, while a J-path avoids reversing the velocity component that was just created upstream. Independent hood support can reduce parasitic coupling, but hood and drum still need controlled compression over their complete angle range. The camera change is evidence that good scoring performance near the hub did not guarantee adequate pose observability in midfield.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Measure path velocity and compression through hood travel, inspect crash loads with the collector extended, and compare localization availability in midfield before and after adding side views. Retain a separate shooter-ready gate before enabling the floor.
