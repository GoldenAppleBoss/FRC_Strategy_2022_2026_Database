# 2023-1678: Simple continuous rigging still requires deliberate line management

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

An Everybot-derived wide roller gripper handles cones and cubes from a common floor pose. A remote chain drives the wrist while keeping its gearbox low. The continuous elevator uses line guides/guards instead of relying solely on a grooved spool; the interview reports many collector iterations.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team explicitly links continuous rigging to fewer strings/failure opportunities and identifies the guards as a way to maintain self-spooling without sacrificing the intended structure.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

A common pickup pose reduces driver and software branching, but different pieces still need different contact/retention behavior. Low motors reduce moving mass while remote transmission adds slack and tension considerations. “Fewer strings” is a mechanism-specific simplification, not proof that rigging is insensitive to line stacking or abrasion.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Run repeated full-travel cycles with realistic line tension, test both pieces at the edges of the acquisition zone, and inspect remote-wrist backlash after impact.

## Limits

Focused technical passages and the retained dossier are the evidence scope. Exact event installation, complete CAD tolerances and all intermediate revisions are not certified.
