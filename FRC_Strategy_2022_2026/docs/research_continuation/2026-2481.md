# 2026-2481: Large inventory changes collector loading, frame clearance and drive gearing

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The interview reports approximately 115-fuel capacity, a 33.5×21.375-inch robot envelope before the described extension, a wide 3-inch two-X60 intake, a load shield above the collector, and hopper structure changed from angle to tube. A two-position hood changed from pneumatic to linear actuation; wheel speed covers intermediate distances. Custom modules allow ratio changes and were geared down for the loaded robot.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The second collector motor and load shield respond to stalling and hopper pressure. Tube structure responds to racking and snagging. The lower drive ratio is explicitly tied to moving a heavily loaded robot across bumps with less stalling/current; the source attributes the actuator change to weight.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

Capacity is useful only if it remains mobile and unloads reliably. A large stored column presses on its lowest contact surfaces, so a shield can be more valuable than another nominally faster roller. The hopper bar cutout and wheel sizing belong to the same route-clearance problem: a robot that can collect a full load but beaches on the bump loses the strategic value of that load. Two hood positions simplify mechanism travel, but the remaining range must be supplied by speed without creating unacceptable release-angle or recovery-time sensitivity.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Test acceleration, bump traversal, braking, tip margin and current at empty/half/full inventory. Measure sustained unload rate rather than dividing nominal capacity by the fastest initial volley. Verify exact dimensions and the source gear notation in CAD.
