# 2026-27: Preacceleration, passive restraint and retrofit compression

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The interview describes a two-X44 intake at 2.5:1, passive carbon upper roller, roller-floor transmission described as 17:19, and a 3-inch steel drum driven by four X60s with powered hood rollers. A previously powered upper feed roller was made passive after jamming. Drum-region compression is reported as 3/8 inch and final roller compression as 1/2 inch.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team explicitly connects the stepped compression to fitting a replacement shooter in the existing robot, and the passive roller change to avoiding fuel being driven downward into jams. Collector repair access is deliberate because it receives impacts.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

The important reference is the sequence of contacts, not simply four launcher motors. Upstream acceleration reduces the velocity increment demanded of the drum, while the passive restraint prevents one contact from injecting fuel into an already occupied region. More powered surfaces can therefore lower useful flow. A retrofit also fixes the available path length and elevation; its compression distribution cannot safely be transplanted without the same incoming velocity and support geometry. Separately, the agitation priority hierarchy means collector deployment and automatic shooting agitation are competing commands rather than independent subsystems.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Measure hopper-to-exit rate and current with the upper restraint passive versus driven, at several fill levels. Check worn fuel and simultaneous agitation. Preserve the source ratio order until driver/driven orientation is verified.
