# 2026-1690: Precondition fuel before a compact turret, then manage defended stability

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The interview describes eleven active carbon/silicone floor rollers powered by an X60 through a stated 2:1 reduction, with 3-mm-pitch GT2 inter-roller belts. A later acceleration stage is described as delivering approximately 8 m/s with near-zero incoming spin. Compact turret packaging shares shafts and places hood-drive hardware to limit diameter. A defended shooting mode cancels shooting on the move.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team explicitly cites efficiency at small pulley radii for its belt choice and compactness for the turret transmission layout. Its defended mode is explained as planting the robot to resist being pushed and emptying more accurately.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

Preconditioning is a useful way to separate high-volume transport from precision release. A turret receiving fuel with controlled velocity and spin needs to add less correction, but it inherits errors in every upstream roller and transfer gap. The reported 8 m/s is an internal-stage claim, not a measured final trajectory specification or match-average rate. A planted mode also shows why turret capability does not require moving continuously: the best control mode depends on contact conditions. A future implementation must budget traction and battery demand for both upstream acceleration and chassis resistance.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Measure entrance velocity/spin distribution, current at each stage, and accuracy under controlled lateral contact. Compare planted and moving modes with the same fuel condition and load rather than ranking them from an undefended demo.
