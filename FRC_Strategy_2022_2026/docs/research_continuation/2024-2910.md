## Published / retained construction account

Under-bumper acquisition feeds four vertical rollers that move the note around the turret’s receiving cage. A light amp guide directs an upward release. The source describes a late, simple worm-drive climb and software superstates that coordinate turret, feeder and launcher rather than binding unrelated commands independently.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team explicitly emphasizes feeding at different turret headings. Climb packaging was constrained by its late addition, motivating simplicity and low mass.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

A turret adds little directional independence if it must return to one heading to acquire every note. The receiving cage and its contact sequence are therefore as important as aiming range. A superstate helps express the common readiness condition, but needs fault handling when only some mechanisms reach their setpoints. A worm transmission should not be assumed to hold every load without checking its actual efficiency and disabled behavior.
