# 2026-9496: A fixed-angle launcher deliberately shifts complexity elsewhere

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The interview describes a passively deploying intake/hopper, roller floor explicitly inspired by 2017 team 254, a separate feed stage, a four-X60 launcher and added brass flywheel inertia. The hood is fixed; distance changes use speed and chassis aim. Intake motion agitates the stored fuel, and the aiming system has a manual fallback.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team explicitly credits the historical floor reference and identifies the fallback as a way to keep playing when automatic aim fails. No team-stated numerical optimum for fixed hood angle is established in this passage.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

Removing hood actuation reduces moving hardware, but does not remove aiming requirements. The usable trajectory envelope is now constrained by wheel speed, launch angle, release height, accuracy and the routes from which the chassis can point at the target. The passive deployment also makes initial orientation and autonomous motion part of the mechanism’s operation. A simple subsystem can therefore demand careful whole-match planning without being a poor design.
