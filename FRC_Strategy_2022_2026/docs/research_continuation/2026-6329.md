# 2026-6329: Roman I and Roman II solve different feed problems

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

Roman I uses a rotating floor/center drum and turret; first-party replies describe a roughly 20.75-inch outer drum and 6-inch center, with no fuel compression. Roman II replaces that path with a full-width roller floor and drum shooter. Its floor pivots about an intake dead shaft for electronics access; long four-bar arms fold under impact. The interview contrasts roughly 8 BPS before the rebuild with a reported 23 afterward.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team ties the rebuild to throughput limitations, the net to substantially greater retained inventory, and compact/flexible intake members to avoiding repeated bending. The release specifically credits 5687’s advice with setting Roman II in motion.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

The strategic change is not simply a faster wheel. Storage, feed width, shooting heading, electrical packaging and intake structure changed together. A comparison should include time spent aiming and avoiding defense, not only an unloaded discharge rate. The original no-compression spindexer and later low-compression drum entry must not be merged into an imaginary hybrid. Service access is especially useful after a rebuild because new routing and wiring require frequent inspection.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Compare complete-cycle output, residual inventory and defended aiming cost for both versions. Verify rate test conditions, and inspect shaft alignment after lifting the floor for service.

## Limits

Focused technical passages and the retained dossier are the evidence scope. Exact event installation, complete CAD tolerances and all intermediate revisions are not certified.
