# 2025-1778: Game simulation preceded the ground-intake commitment

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

A narrow initial ground concept became a wide collector, feeding an internally belted elevator and arm that can score toward either side. The team reports offseason experience with similar mechanisms. Each software state specifies the collector, elevator, arm and roller setpoints together, with explicit transition conditions.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team describes running match simulations on day two after kickoff to test the significance of unprotected station defense. It connects the floor collector to that strategic result and the monolithic state structure to predictable safe motion.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

This is a process reference as much as a hardware reference. The team tested a game constraint before committing to a cycle architecture, then used known mechanism families to reduce development risk. Wide acquisition helps only when the handoff can keep up and the arm can safely retract past both field and robot structure. A multi-subsystem state should retain possession information so a timing variation does not silently drop coral.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Repeat the strategic simulation with plausible opposing and partner robots. Test interrupted transfers and safe recovery from each intermediate state, rather than validating only the nominal score sequence.

## Limits

Focused technical passages and the retained dossier are the evidence scope. Exact event installation, complete CAD tolerances and all intermediate revisions are not certified.
