# 2026-5687: Capstan deployment and belt-floor lanes are dated fixes, not universal upgrades

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

First-party replies describe 2-inch upper rollers, a 1.25-inch lower roller and about half an inch of compression. The robot changed from roller floor to belt floor between URI and UVM, and from rack deployment to a rope capstan after tooth damage. A 6-inch, 1/8-inch-wall aluminum drum was urethane-coated, machined and balanced; its reported inertia is a little over 80 lb·in².

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team says lower collector compression reduced motor bogging. It reports lower current and faster feeding after the belt-floor change; belt spacing created lanes and reduced fuel spinning in place. Rope deployment addressed rack tooth failures, with rope tension becoming the continuing adjustment.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

This is a useful counterexample to choosing a floor from appearance alone. Belt spacing can organize deformable fuel and remove an unproductive rotating contact state, but that result depends on piece size, belt spacing, load and friction. A capstan cannot skip discrete gear teeth, yet it can slip, stretch, lose tension or suffer anchor damage. The team’s prior capstan experience also changes development risk: the decision was supported by an existing implementation history rather than an untested mechanism adopted casually midseason.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Test lane stability, residual fuel, electrical demand and rope tension after repeated impacts. Preserve the report that auto shot timers shortened by over half a second without inventing exact before/after BPS.
