# 2026-7769: Passive feed solved one jam while rack durability remained unresolved

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The rack collector and belt floor feed an originally divided shooter that became one continuous drum. In the team’s later technical discussion, powered upper/lower indexing rollers overpacked the ramp; immobilizing the lower rollers corrected that feed issue. The static hood avoids an exposed powered-roller belt. The team separately reports repeated polycarbonate rack-tooth failures and a 45-A deploying/5-A deployed current-limit strategy at Worlds.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The stated priorities are simple, durable mechanisms and resistance to trench contact. The lower surface was made static to meter entry rather than forcing fuel into a congested ramp. The current-limit change aimed to let the intake collapse under impact; failures became less frequent but did not disappear.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

This example needs two distinct failure histories. A feed-path change can be successful while the deployment structure remains unreliable. Stronger propulsion is not automatically the fix for a packing jam, and a high-performing alliance run does not certify rack durability. Source-reported storage also has a useful distinction: almost 70 geometric capacity versus commonly 50–60 in play. Protected interior PETG vector wheels surviving a season is not evidence that the same prints would survive as exposed intake wheels.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Test the passive lower feed with full hopper pressure and varied fuel. Instrument deployment impact/current and inspect tooth-root damage. A proposed decoupling of intake sides is a future idea, not a fielded fix.
