# 2025-190: Funnel closure and adaptive grip are separate control problems

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

A retroreflective beam across the station funnel detects entry and triggers closure before coral rolls to the end effector. An extending roller acquires ground algae. The retained scoring assembly uses an elevator and arm; the climb deploys with a gas shock and captures the cage while the robot advances.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The interview describes varying algae-holding voltage using current-related feedback, with more effort when the piece is farther out and less after it is seated. Exact thresholds are not supplied.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

An entry beam establishes interruption, not orientation or secure final possession. The handoff therefore needs additional state and a recovery path. Adaptive grip can reduce unnecessary loading after capture, but current also changes with friction, compression and voltage; it cannot be treated as a direct position measurement without calibration. The gas-shock climb similarly depends on a defined hard stop and a controlled chassis approach.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Induce partial entry and bounced coral, measure algae retention at different compression and current, and verify cage capture over realistic lateral offsets.
