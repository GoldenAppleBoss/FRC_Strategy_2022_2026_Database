# 2024-1778: Minimal intake rollers and redundant climb capacity have different justifications

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The over-bumper collector uses four rollers after early prototyping. An indexer retains the note and serves amp delivery through a virtual linkage. The top/bottom launcher uses asymmetric wheel contact. Each climber has two motors, and the team describes continuing after one side was removed following a failure.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team ties the reduced collector to sufficient pickup performance with lower mass, and the unusual climb power to single-side operation and imperfect chain alignment. Asymmetric contact is reported to improve spin/stability and range.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

Simplicity should be evaluated per task rather than imposed uniformly. Removing an unnecessary roller can improve collector dynamics, while adding climb reserve can preserve a match outcome after damage. Those are compatible engineering decisions. Single-side climbing also changes structural loads and roll, so redundancy is only real if the frame and scoring mechanisms tolerate the alternate load case.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Test the minimum collector with worn notes and wall contact. Validate disabled-side climb loading and harmony clearance, rather than extrapolating from two-side nominal lifts.

Focused technical passages and the retained dossier are the evidence scope. Exact event installation, complete CAD tolerances and all intermediate revisions are not certified.
