# 2026-125: Dish packaging and shooting independence require a coordinated robot

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The interview describes an eleven-position dish, front and rear vertical roller sets to clear its center, approximately 425 degrees of turret freedom, a 3-inch main shooter wheel, a 1-inch upper roller and additional feed wheels. A third X44 was added for difficult passes and corner shots. Intake and drivetrain packaging are shaped around the large dish.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team identifies moving shots, shooting under defense and simultaneous acquisition as reasons to retain the turret architecture. The doubled vertical rollers address fuel left in the dish center; the extra shooter motor addresses range/power demand.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

A centrally packaged turret is a whole-robot commitment. The dish determines where modules, structure and storage can sit, and its useful capacity depends on clearing the central dead region rather than nominal perimeter volume. Turret range is only useful if localization, target selection, hood travel and cable management support it. The FMS-aware timing offset is a flight-time decision: fuel released before activation must arrive while scoring is valid. That offset is not a transferable constant without checking release-to-arrival time and the applicable game timing.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Compare central residual fuel, sustained rate and moving-shot accuracy at different turret angles. Validate the hub-state lead time against measured flight time; verify cable/swept-volume clearance over the claimed angular range.
