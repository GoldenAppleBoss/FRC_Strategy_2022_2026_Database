# 2025-180: Self-deploying acquisition still requires indexed possession

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

A compliant four-bar floor collector feeds a funnel and rotating effector on a two-stage continuous elevator. The retained interview describes reverse roller action deploying the collector. One collector sensor and three arm sensors manage entry, indexing and retention; camera-based acquisition chooses a robot pose relative to the pickup.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team connects floor pickup to shorter coral cycles and describes automatic reef-facing behavior as a way to streamline the driver’s next scoring action. It sought both task-related and climb ranking points.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

Passive or roller-driven deployment reduces a dedicated actuator but makes friction, game-piece contact and the initial mechanism state part of the deployment logic. Multiple sensors provide intermediate possession states instead of equating “something entered” with “ready to score.” A combined coral/algae mechanism must also preserve the retained object through rotation, not just when stationary.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Test partial handoffs, skewed coral, deployment without a piece, and failures of each sensor. Verify motor identifiers in CAD/code because automatic captions corrupt model names.

## Limits

Focused technical passages and the retained dossier are the evidence scope. Exact event installation, complete CAD tolerances and all intermediate revisions are not certified.
