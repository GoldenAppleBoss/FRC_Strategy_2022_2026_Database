# 2024-2974: Pivot-settle delay and belt skipping require dated evidence

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The build thread records replacing a slower, note-damaging conveyor with a more direct curved feed, carbon-roller shooter changes, replaceable intake assemblies and pivot stiffening. Later posts describe a friction damper, changing pivot belts, a ratio change from 125:1 to 100:1, and Worlds logs exposing belt skipping when the arm extended.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team explicitly ties the direct feed to speed/note preservation, the damper to waiting several seconds for the angle to settle, and the revised ratio to faster rotation. Log comparison between motor and external encoder exposed a mechanical slip rather than merely a software aiming error.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

This history explains why commanded angle, measured motor angle and true launcher angle are different quantities. Increasing control gains cannot reliably fix a slipping transmission. The fastest pivot also is not the one with the shortest complete aim-to-shot time if it oscillates afterward. The early proposal to climb using the shooter must remain a proposal unless later installation evidence supports it.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Compare motor and external encoder trajectories under arm extension, measure request-to-exit latency, and inspect intake damage after contact.
