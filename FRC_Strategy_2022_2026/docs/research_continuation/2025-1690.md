# 2025-1690: Differential arm/elevator motion keeps motors low but couples control

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The source describes an efficient floor collector, V-shaped powered centering path and a differential elevator/arm transmission. Equal coordinated belt motion translates the carriage; differential motion changes arm angle while the motors stay low. The retained technical record also describes a custom-sealed suction effector and piston/scotch-yoke vacuum source.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team’s explicit objective is short, fast coral cycles and resistance to defense without excessive sacrifice of algae capability. Keeping arm/elevator drive motors at the base serves low moving mass.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

A differential does not eliminate shoulder or elevator effort; it redistributes that demand across shared actuators. Motion limits, current and saturation must be considered in joint space and motor space. The vacuum end effector can reduce exposed gripping hardware, but seal geometry and surface condition become critical. A short-cycle strategy also needs coordinated human-player supply and a path that avoids interference with partners.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Test simultaneous lift/rotation near motor limits, vacuum retention on damaged or wet pieces, and recovery after a partially seated coral. Preserve emergency climb acceleration as a high-load exception, not the normal durability baseline.
