# 2025-3683: Station retention, orientation recovery and fast lift must work together

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

A post-Niagara ramp/retention change reduces coral bounce-out and permits longer-distance station delivery. Two sensors support orientation detection and unjamming, while the ramp can queue coral during algae possession. The internally belted three-stage elevator has two low motors and tie-rod stiffening; the team reports slightly over half a second to full extension.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The source ties the ramp revision directly to entry bounce and the tie rods to rigidity. The retained record describes using an upper roller to redirect coral for L1 as well as higher-level scoring.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

A fast unloaded lift is valuable only if the queued piece remains controlled and the effector settles accurately. Keeping the next coral on the ramp while holding algae can remove travel from the next cycle, but requires distinct possession states and a deliberate handoff. The reported lift time should remain a mechanism claim rather than a full-cycle time.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Measure loaded settle time, queued-piece retention during lift motion, and unjamming success for sideways coral. Check cable retention over repeated rapid extensions.

## Limits

Focused technical passages and the retained dossier are the evidence scope. Exact event installation, complete CAD tolerances and all intermediate revisions are not certified.
