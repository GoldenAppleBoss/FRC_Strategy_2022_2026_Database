# 2024-3005: Contact geometry and fast wheel service both affect available scoring

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

Front/rear intake paths stage a note on low-friction support and feed a pivoting transfer. The launcher uses four Vortex-driven contact groups after tests with fewer contacts. The interview also discusses rapidly replaceable printed wheel components, reporting swaps in under a minute when a replacement is prepared.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team says earlier arrangements did not provide sufficient contact and that additional roller power/placement emerged through testing. Serviceability was deliberately considered for playoff repair windows.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

Motor count is the consequence of a contact requirement, not a standalone design target. Each contact must accelerate without deforming the note into a less repeatable state. Repair speed affects how much performance survives a tournament: a fast but fragile part may be viable only if replacement stock and access are designed at the same time.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Compare slip, note deformation and release speed across contact arrangements. Time an actual module replacement with match-ready tools and verify the resulting alignment.
