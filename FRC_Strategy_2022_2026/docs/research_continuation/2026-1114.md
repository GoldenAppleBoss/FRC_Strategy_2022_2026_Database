# 2026-1114: A rising fuel path makes agitation and service mass consequential

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The collector kicker changed from a hanging deployed bar to a member that folds with the hopper. Side agitation, passive dead-zone rollers, added gripping rollers and a belt-linked roller elevator feed the drum. The retained dated source account records the climber being installed March 1 and removed March 19 before the first event.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The folding kicker is explained as improving bump clearance. Added contact surfaces respond to trapped fuel and insufficient feed. The retained team account attributes removing the climber to weight, with later mass devoted to reliable feeding.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

This design transports fuel upward before release, so every additional elevation change has a traction and packing cost. An apparently small passive roller may be important because it removes a place where the moving stream can unload into a dead pocket. The coupled hopper/kicker motion is also a route-clearance feature, not merely storage expansion. Removing a climber should be understood as reallocating mass and development effort to the bottleneck; it does not imply that tower scoring was mechanically impossible or that endgame fuel should be counted as climb value.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Log current and residual inventory along the entire rising path, test folding while fully loaded, and compare bump clearance before and after the kicker folds. Do not derive undisclosed gear ratios from the interview wording.
