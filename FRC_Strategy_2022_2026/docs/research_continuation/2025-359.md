# 2025-359: A telescoping arm avoids a handoff but moves a large work envelope

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

The interview describes four shoulder motors with a stated 111:1 reduction, a chain-driven dead-axis pivot, and a two-motor telescoping arm. Its wide collector uses front/back roller groups and aluminum centering guides to acquire differently oriented coral; rigid guide plates reflect coral’s low compressibility. The retained record describes extending stage length for L4/net reach.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team links the multi-motor shoulder to power demand. Its acquisition geometry is intended to bring floor coral into the same mechanism that scores it, avoiding a separate transfer.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

Eliminating a handoff trades transfer risk for arm inertia, sweep clearance and control burden. Extension changes both shoulder torque and the position of the robot’s loaded center of mass. A floor hard stop can define a repeatable pickup plane, but should not be confused with clearance throughout the rest of the motion. Thin coral tolerates less forced compression than foam fuel, so roller selection must be paired with a controlled guide gap.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Map shoulder current and deflection through extension, test pickup against a wall, and verify coral control during acceleration and sudden chassis braking.
