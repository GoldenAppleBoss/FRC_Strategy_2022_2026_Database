# 2025-604: Precision depends on stiffness, backlash and feeding order

Focused study supplement. Not a full binder/CAD review, exhaustive subsystem specification, every-version history or visual interview watch.

## Published / retained construction account

A single-stage elevator is driven by chains on both sides to reduce twist; the arm/wrist and shimmed transmissions aim to minimize backlash. The large corrugated-plastic station funnel damps entry. Independently stopped feed rollers can hold coral while the gripper handles algae, and a compact climber fits beneath the funnel.

## Team-stated reasoning or boundary

The team explicitly treats accuracy as a mechanical and software requirement, identifies dual-sided elevator drive with resisting twist, and explains pausing coral feed during algae possession.

## Engineering interpretation — analyst inference

A vision system can command the correct pose yet miss a branch if the scoring frame deflects or the transmission reverses through backlash. Hybrid cycles add a scheduling constraint: the incoming coral must not collide with a manipulator that is still gripping algae. The funnel and climber also compete for the same physical space, so a copied climber cannot be assumed to fit behind another team’s station system.

## Proposed validation — not performed

Measure effector displacement under lateral load and direction reversal; test queued coral while holding algae. Compare repeatability after repeated elevator cycles and drivetrain impacts.
