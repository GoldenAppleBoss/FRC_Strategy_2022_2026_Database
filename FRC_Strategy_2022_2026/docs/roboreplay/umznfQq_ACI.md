## Retained full-caption account

The commentator follows 1678 after an intake failure: test remaining acquisition, then switch to defense on major scorers. Flat bumper contact, wall leverage and release timing matter. Late denial depends on whether the opponent still has time for another cycle and balance. A suspected module failure is commentary, not a verified diagnosis.

## 1. Diagnose quickly, then change role

[Navigation 110–175 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umznfQq_ACI&t=110)

**Source account / attributed commentary:** The source describes 1678 testing cube and cone acquisition after a collector failure, then promptly becoming a defender when neither works.

**Conditional interpretation — analyst reasoning:** The decision variable is remaining useful capability. A short diagnostic sequence can prevent spending the rest of teleop attempting an impossible task. The value of defense rises when it replaces zero scoring rather than a productive cycle.

## 2. Maintain a useful bumper relationship

[Navigation 329–394 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umznfQq_ACI&t=329)

**Source account / attributed commentary:** The commentator praises rotating with 3005 while leaving enough separation to avoid being flung out of position by mismatched rotation. He suspects a module problem later.

**Conditional interpretation — analyst reasoning:** “Stay in contact” is too crude a defensive instruction. Relative angle, wall position, escape routes and the timing of recontact matter. The module diagnosis is a commentator hypothesis, not verified mechanical causation.

## 3. Block the acquisition face and evaluate time remaining

[Navigation 548–613 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umznfQq_ACI&t=548)

**Source account / attributed commentary:** The account describes parallel blocking of 1114’s pickup face, forcing a longer approach near the end of the match.

**Conditional interpretation — analyst reasoning:** A defender need not stop a robot forever. It may need only to make one remaining cycle infeasible before balancing. Exact effectiveness requires checking the match clock, remaining tasks and penalties, not merely counting contact seconds.
