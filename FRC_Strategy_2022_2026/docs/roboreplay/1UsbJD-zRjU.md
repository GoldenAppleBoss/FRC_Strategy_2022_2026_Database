## 1. Kickoff: rank incentives differ from point value

[Navigation 150–395 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1UsbJD-zRjU&t=150)

**Source account / attributed commentary:** The commentator compares match points with the ease of earning different ranking points. In his retrospective, leave-plus-one-coral and a deep climb plus park appear relatively accessible, making coral-level completion an important qualifier differentiator.

**Conditional interpretation — analyst reasoning:** This is a prediction framework tied to a field of teams, not an immutable task ranking. If a bonus becomes routine, its marginal ranking value falls even though its point value is unchanged. Evaluate the expected event population and rule revision before allocating mechanism effort.

## 2. Why algae looked unattractive early

[Navigation 600–665 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1UsbJD-zRjU&t=600)

**Source account / attributed commentary:** The recap explains why some teams initially discounted processor play: scoring could also deliver algae to the opponent, whose human player might convert it. It contrasts that with the apparent availability and speed of coral cycles.

**Conditional interpretation — analyst reasoning:** Expected net value depends on the receiving human player and the opponent’s recovery opportunity. Treating all processor actions as a fixed two-point gain, or all algae as poor value, erases those conditions. A kickoff judgment can be reasonable and still become obsolete as skills and congestion change.

## 4. Floor supply changes cycle geography

[Navigation 1120–1185 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1UsbJD-zRjU&t=1120)

**Source account / attributed commentary:** The source contrasts an early defensive third robot with another scorer sharing limited space. It then identifies 1690’s human-player floor supply as reducing travel toward the reef.

**Conditional interpretation — analyst reasoning:** The architecture must accept a supply distribution, not a guaranteed piece at a fixed point. The third robot’s best role depends on marginal scoring, interference and defense opportunity; a robot with low standalone output may be useful without occupying another scorer’s lane.

## 5. 1323 spends surplus coral to save time

[Navigation 1370–1435 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1UsbJD-zRjU&t=1370)

**Source account / attributed commentary:** The commentator describes a more aggressive field-supply approach: many coral pieces are delivered, collisions help move them forward, and some leftovers are accepted because the alliance does not need to score every available piece.

**Conditional interpretation — analyst reasoning:** Unused inventory is not necessarily waste if it buys shorter cycles. This changes the pickup objective from perfect recovery to fast acquisition of any convenient piece. It also creates traffic, visibility and finite-supply questions that should be tested before copying the method at a different skill level.

## 6. Backside autonomous protects partner access

[Navigation 1620–1685 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1UsbJD-zRjU&t=1620)

**Source account / attributed commentary:** The recap credits 2910 as the example the author remembers, while expressly declining to establish who invented the route. A fast backside route lets partners retain straightforward station access.

**Conditional interpretation — analyst reasoning:** An autonomous routine should be valued by alliance output and route compatibility, not only its own piece count. Ground pickup of predictably positioned pieces can make a longer-looking route more reliable. Keep observed example, claimed priority and invention credit separate.

## 7. Finite algae produces a score swing

[Navigation 1850–1915 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1UsbJD-zRjU&t=1850)

**Source account / attributed commentary:** The source explains that when branch output is similar, a missed algae can deprive one alliance of a net score and give the opponent the corresponding opportunity.

**Conditional interpretation — analyst reasoning:** The eight-point potential swing is conditional: both sides must otherwise have converted the contested piece. Do not double-count denial and a scored piece as two independent guaranteed gains. The important generalization is that a finite contested resource can determine the match after another scoring channel becomes similar.
