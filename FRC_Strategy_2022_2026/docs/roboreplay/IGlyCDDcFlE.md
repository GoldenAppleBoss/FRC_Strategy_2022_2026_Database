## 1. Choose the pass disruption target

[Navigation 149–214 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IGlyCDDcFlE&t=149)

**Source account / attributed commentary:** The source describes 1729 switching among passers and using wall leverage, while 4613 escapes by backing up and rotating.

**Conditional interpretation — analyst reasoning:** A fixed assignment to the strongest opponent is not always best. The defender should consider escape skill, replacement supply and how much disruption can actually be sustained.

## 2. Poaching can combine scoring with denial

[Navigation 448–513 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IGlyCDDcFlE&t=448)

**Source account / attributed commentary:** The commentator contrasts costly source-zone contact with stealing a note that would otherwise have been passed. He describes that theft as a two-note opportunity swing.

**Conditional interpretation — analyst reasoning:** The swing is conditional on both alliances’ ability to use the piece. Penalties can erase the gain. A hybrid defender/poacher needs a low-detour acquisition route, not only a strong chassis.

## 3. Release a losing contest for a timed amplification opportunity

[Navigation 746–811 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IGlyCDDcFlE&t=746)

**Source account / attributed commentary:** Late in the analysis, the defender leaves a risky contest to collect a dropped note while the alliance has an amplification opportunity.

**Conditional interpretation — analyst reasoning:** Defensive persistence can be strategically wrong when the next offensive contribution has higher marginal value. The passage connects remaining clock, penalty risk, inventory and amplifier readiness; it is not a general instruction to abandon passing defense late.
