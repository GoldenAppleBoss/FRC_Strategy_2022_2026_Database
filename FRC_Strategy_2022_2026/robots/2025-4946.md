## Alpha Dogs: retention, compliance and source fallback

Version: 2025 robot. [PDF p7](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/uploads/short-url/1EXbd0W1SarZod0NlxAfatGysac.pdf#page=7) · [PDF p8](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/uploads/short-url/1EXbd0W1SarZod0NlxAfatGysac.pdf#page=8)

**Published construction:** Single roller became dual; long compliance linkage became compact sprung arm; first event used station funnel.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Solve escaping coral, collision vulnerability and packaging.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** Capturing a tube is not the same as retaining it after the first contact. The second roller can close an escape path while increasing the need to control compression. An impact-yielding linkage can protect the pickup, but a long arm consumes chassis volume and increases bending moment. The report’s station-funnel fallback is evidence of a competition configuration, while its ground-intake drawings include developmental versions. Keep those distinct when recommending inspiration: a promising prototype is not automatically a demonstrated event mechanism.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Test retention during braking and reverse motion; verify the exact event configuration before using a version as a performance example.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## Alpha Dogs: conveyor and tray progressively consolidated

Version: 2025 robot. [PDF p8](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/uploads/short-url/1EXbd0W1SarZod0NlxAfatGysac.pdf#page=8) · [PDF p9](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/uploads/short-url/1EXbd0W1SarZod0NlxAfatGysac.pdf#page=9)

**Published construction:** Opposed star-wheel conveyors evolved into a lighter polycarbonate assembly and integrated receiving cup.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Straighten coral and simplify the transfer structure.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** A conveyor that both straightens and transports a tube can avoid a separate orientation stage, but its downstream tray must stop the tube without sending it back into the drive contacts. Removing redundant support material can improve access and mass while changing alignment under load. A receiving cup reduces uncertainty in the arm’s pickup position, provided it has no lip that prevents release into the gripper. The best architecture depends on the handoff’s repeatability and recovery after a partial acquisition, not only its nominal travel time.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Test varied tube orientation and arrival speed, then interrupt the handoff to verify that the mechanism can recover without driver intervention.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## Alpha Dogs: gripper envelope changed the architecture

Version: 2025 robot. [PDF p10](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/uploads/short-url/1EXbd0W1SarZod0NlxAfatGysac.pdf#page=10)

**Published construction:** Combined piece gripper revised after reach/envelope problems; elevator-mounted arrangement developed.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Keep the placing assembly within the intended geometry.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** Adding a second piece function changes more than roller spacing: the swept volume, center of mass and necessary approach path may all grow. A gripper that works on a bench can violate the robot’s usable envelope when attached to an arm. Moving it onto an elevator changes the kinematic problem but does not by itself prove accurate release. The report leaves some final geometry and transmission details unresolved, so an agent should retrieve CAD or later event evidence rather than invent an elevator ratio or a final mechanism sequence.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Check the full loaded piece envelope and actual event revision; measure pickup, hold and release separately for each piece.

**Limits:** This engineering report documents development. It does not establish every final CAD dimension, elevator reduction or competition use of every pictured prototype.

## Alpha Dogs: reduce the cage mechanism to its useful load path

Version: 2025 robot. [PDF p6](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/uploads/short-url/1EXbd0W1SarZod0NlxAfatGysac.pdf#page=6)

**Published construction:** Complex capture concepts led to a simpler pivoting jack; chain endpoint targeted the robot’s mass center.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Reduce weight and improve hanging geometry.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** A cage mechanism must transfer the robot’s weight through a secure contact while allowing the robot to settle into a stable orientation. A design can capture well yet demand excessive torque because its chain line remains far from the center of mass. Simplifying to a lever reduces parts but can concentrate load at the main pivot and rope attachment. The analysis should identify the actual hanging geometry and load path, with an explicit boundary between proposed and competed revisions.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Test off-center acquisition and load transition; confirm the final revision with dated match or pit evidence.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## Alpha Dogs: camera view and reef occupancy are architecture inputs

Version: 2025 robot. [PDF p12](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/uploads/short-url/1EXbd0W1SarZod0NlxAfatGysac.pdf#page=12) · [PDF p17](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/uploads/short-url/1EXbd0W1SarZod0NlxAfatGysac.pdf#page=17) · [PDF p18](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/uploads/short-url/1EXbd0W1SarZod0NlxAfatGysac.pdf#page=18) · [PDF p19](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/uploads/short-url/1EXbd0W1SarZod0NlxAfatGysac.pdf#page=19) · [PDF p20](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/uploads/short-url/1EXbd0W1SarZod0NlxAfatGysac.pdf#page=20)

**Published construction:** Camera angle revised for close tags; field-region alignment and operator-entered reef occupancy are described.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Reduce blind spots and repeated target-selection work.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** A camera can see an AprilTag far away yet lose it at the exact distance where precision is needed. Mount angle and obstruction therefore belong in the early CAD review. Keeping reef occupancy in software changes the operator’s task from repeatedly aiming to maintaining a reliable shared state. Incorrect occupancy can send the robot toward a filled branch, so the system needs a quick correction and manual override. The mechanism recommendation should include what target information it assumes the controls will supply.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Test tag visibility at every final scoring pose and recovery from deliberately incorrect occupancy entries.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.
