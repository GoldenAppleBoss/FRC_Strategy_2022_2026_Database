# 2024-254: technical mechanisms, design reasoning and revision boundaries

These notes distinguish published construction from team-stated intent and original engineering inference. Selected numerical values are reported source data, not independently reproduced measurements. The recommended tests have not been performed. A binder snapshot does not certify every competition version.

## VORTEX: centered under-bumper acquisition

Version: VORTEX. [PDF p11](https://media.team254.com/2024/10/8f2607a0-2024-Tech-Binder.pdf#page=11)

**Published construction:** Mecanum front contact, kicker rollers and angled powered funneling wheels.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Accept side approaches and center notes without jamming.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** A note arriving sideways or off-center should be translated toward the feeder before the shooter demands a repeatable entry state. Mecanum and angled contacts can supply that transverse velocity, but excess lateral force can fold or wedge the note. A stiff bumper and intake mount preserve the intended compression under defense. The correct performance measure includes successful ownership from the edge of the mouth, not only straight-on pickup at the center.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Map pickup across the mouth with deformed notes, oblique approaches and bumper impacts; observe the first region where motion stops.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## VORTEX: annular feed removes the intake-indexing heading constraint

Version: VORTEX. [PDF p12](https://media.team254.com/2024/10/8f2607a0-2024-Tech-Binder.pdf#page=12) · [PDF p13](https://media.team254.com/2024/10/8f2607a0-2024-Tech-Binder.pdf#page=13)

**Published construction:** Two-direction annular feeder supplies a turret without returning it to the intake; 720° cable system.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Maintain goal tracking during acquisition and feeding.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** The architectural benefit is not merely a large turret angle. Many turreted designs must rotate to an intake alignment before receiving a note, losing continuous tracking. An annular feed trades that delay for a larger guided path whose friction, seams and direction switching must be managed. Choosing the shorter route can reduce feed time, while a reversal must preserve piece state. The cable system makes the aiming envelope physically usable; without reliable wire guidance, the extra angular range is only a CAD possibility.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Feed at multiple turret headings including through a direction change; test note wear, guide friction and cable-chain operation near both limits.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## VORTEX: revised shooter separates feed and launch contacts

Version: VORTEX. [PDF p14](https://media.team254.com/2024/10/8f2607a0-2024-Tech-Binder.pdf#page=14)

**Published construction:** Second-revision quad-wheel shooter; staged feed rollers; asymmetrical wheels; sector-gear hood.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Controlled note entry and spin for a flatter useful trajectory.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** The note’s flexible ring makes its entry orientation and deformation important to launch repeatability. A dedicated first stage can establish the approach before the high-speed wheels impart the final velocity and spin. Asymmetric contact can bias spin, but the resulting flight still needs empirical validation over wear and range. Hood backlash is an angular error that becomes more consequential at some distances than others. The revision label matters: a final shooter image must not silently stand in for an earlier competition version.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Measure entry deformation and shot dispersion with several note conditions; compare hood approaches from both directions and both feed routes.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## VORTEX: amplifier is a second routing destination

Version: VORTEX. [PDF p15](https://media.team254.com/2024/10/8f2607a0-2024-Tech-Binder.pdf#page=15)

**Published construction:** Roller carriage on an elevator routes notes to amp/trap and accepts human-player feed.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Support multiple scoring and shuttling roles.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** The amplifier is not just a final scoring roller. It adds a branch to the note-routing state machine and a distinct human-player acquisition path. Shared use can justify the mechanism’s mass if those roles are valuable in the alliance plan, but creates more transitions and collision states. Passive trap-reaction wheels supply a structural contact during climbing, tying its geometry to the endgame sequence. The agent should explain those dependencies before recommending the package to a team that only needs one of its functions.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Test every routing transition and interrupted sequence; verify that the trap reaction contact does not dislodge the stored note.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.

## VORTEX: rapid rope climb establishes trap height

Version: VORTEX. [PDF p16](https://media.team254.com/2024/10/8f2607a0-2024-Tech-Binder.pdf#page=16)

**Published construction:** Sprung hook arms and a low-mounted rope winch pull the robot to trap height.

**Team-stated reason or evidence boundary:** Lift quickly while enabling the amplifier to reach the trap.

**Engineering analysis — original inference:** The climb’s lift distance is determined by the scorer’s required geometry, not just the minimum suspension score. Gas springs can deploy the hooks while the winch retracts them under load. A rope path needs secure anchors, consistent winding and sufficient clearance as the robot swings. If retention relies on drivetrain or motor braking behavior, that exact power-off state should be validated rather than assumed. The trap sequence must preserve the reaction contact and note orientation throughout the lift.

**Proposed validation — not a completed test:** Test acquisition, lift, trap alignment and disable as one sequence with several chain heights and swing angles.

**Limits:** The binder is a published configuration snapshot, not an independent durability or match-rate test.
